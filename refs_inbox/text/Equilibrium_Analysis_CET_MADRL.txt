Abstract—With the proposal of China's carbon peaking and 
carbon neutrality goals, carbon emission trading (CET) is 
gradually participating in the electricity market to accelerate 
carbon emission reduction and the improvement of power supply 
structure. In this study, we analyze the impacts of CET on the 
electricity market based on the electricity market equilibrium 
model and multi-agent deep reinforcement learning (MADL) 
method. We firstly establish the electricity market clearing 
process involved CET and develop the bi-level problem to model 
the electricity market equilibrium with strategic generation 
company (GENCO) bidders. Then, a multi-agent Twin Delayed 
Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (MATD3) algorithm is 
applied to solve the market equilibrium described above. Finally, 
we simulate multiple cases based on a modified IEEE 30-bus 
system. The result shows that an excessive carbon price can raise 
the nodal electricity price and have a negative influence on 
reducing carbon emission, and an appropriately low carbon 
emission quota setting can help for carbon emission reduction. 
Keywords—electricity market equilibrium, carbon emission 
trading, multi-agent deep reinforcement learning, carbon price, 
carbon quota 
I. INTRODUCTION  
Energy and environmental issues are becoming increasingly 
serious, and China's carbon emissions and energy consumption 
are the highest in the world. To accelerate carbon mitigation and 
achieve sustainable development, China has proposed to achieve 
carbon peak and carbon neutrality goals by 2030 and 2060 
respectively, and the construction of carbon emission trading 
(CET) market is gradually advancing [1]. The carbon emission 
trading market is a market mechanism that allocates the total 
amount of permitted carbon emissions within a certain time and 
space to carbon emitters in the form of allowances, and allows 
carbon emission rights to be traded as commodity. The purpose 
of CET mechanism is to reduce carbon emissions, improve 
energy efficiency, and optimize the power supply structure of 
the industries involved. The market-oriented carbon price is 
formed to guide market participants to reduce carbon emissions 
proactively. Meanwhile, the carbon emissions of the power 
industry are very large, CET market will significantly affect the 
cost of electricity for generation companies (GENCOs) [2]. In 
the context of carbon neutrality goal and electricity market 
reform, CET will have a great influence on the equilibrium of 
electricity market. 
The electricity equilibrium problem, also known as the 
strategic electricity market bidding problem, is essentially 
finding the Nash equilibrium point of the electricity market. The 
electricity market bidding problem can be formulated as a bi-
level model, the upper-level model is the power producers profit 
maximization problem, and the lower-level model is the 
electricity market clearing model with the goal of minimizing 
total power generation costs. A widely applied method to deal 
with the bi-level problem above is using the lower-level model's 
Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) optimality conditions to convert it 
to a single-level mathematical problem with equilibrium 
constraints (MPEC) [3]. The combination of all strategic 
GENCO bidders’ MPEC formulates the equilibrium problem 
with equilibrium constraints (EPEC), which can be solved by 
applying multiple linearization methods or using the 
diagonalization algorithm [4], [5]. 
All the above work can be classified as model-driven 
approaches, which mainly rely on the assumption that each 
GENCO bidder has the complete information of other bidders’

This paper proposes a electricity market GENCO bidding

model includes a GENCO bidder optimal strategy model and an 
electricity market clearing model considering the CET market. 
Then, a state of the art reinforcement learning algorithm is used 
to find the Nash equilibrium of the electricity market. Finally, 
multiple cases are simulated to study the effects of carbon 
emission quota and carbon price on the electricity market 
equilibrium.

II. ELECTRICITY MARKET EQUILIBRIUM MODEL 
A. Market Framework 
The market proposed in this paper includes a single-period 
electricity spot market and a carbon emission trading market. 
Firstly, each GENCO bidder offers its bidding price to the 
independent system operator (ISO), the price can be the true cost 
of its power generation or a higher strategic offer to obtain more 
profit. Then the ISO clears the market based on the offers of all 
GENCO bidders and the parameters of the power network. The 
nodal electricity price of each node and the output of each 
GENCO are provided from market clearing process, then the 
carbon emission of each GENCO during power generation and 
its profit from electricity market can be calculated. Compared 
individual’s carbon emissions with the free carbon emission 
quota, each GENCO sells all remaining carbon emission rights 
or buys missing carbon emission rights from the CET market. 
After the CET market clearing, the GENCO can obtain its profit 
from CET market, and calculate its total profit during the market 
clearing process. It is worth noting that GENCOs just offer the 
generation cost in electricity market strategically, they don’t 
need to decide how much of the remaining carbon emission 
rights to sell in CET market. 
The bi-level model of electricity market equilibrium 
problem includes the upper-level model for GENCO, and the 
lower-level model for market clearing process, which is 
formulated in the following.

III. MULTI-AGENT DEEP REINFORCEMENT LEARNING 
ALGORITHM 
A MATD3 approach is proposed to solve the electricity 
market equilibrium problem formulated in Section II. TD3 is a 
reinforcement learning algorithm [8], which has 
the 
characteristics of model-free, off-policy and continuous state-
action space. TD3 is applicable to deal with the single-agent 
decision-making problem. MATD3 is an extension of TD3 
algorithm considering the dynamic environment when multiple 
agents make decision at the same time, which is applicable to 
solve the electricity market equilibrium problem with multiple 
GENCO bidders bidding. 
A. Multi-agent Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy 
Gradient Approach 
Multi-agent deep deterministic policy gradient (MADDPG) 
is a commonly used multi-agent deep reinforcement learning 
algorithm [9]. However, MADDPG has the problems of 
overestimation and high variance. MATD3 is designed to solve 
the problems above [10]. To solve the overestimation problem, 
the critic of TD3 consists of two Q-networks and two target Q-
networks to estimate the Q value independently. The smaller Q 
value is chosen to calculate and update networks, which 
effectively alleviates the overestimation. Delayed policy update 
and target policy smoothing regularization are applied to deal 
with the high variance problem. Delayed policy update approach 
reduces the update frequency to improve the learning stability of 
actor network, and target policy smoothing regularization 
approach adds Gaussian noise when updating the critic network 
to estimate the Q value more accurately and enhance the 
robustness of the network. 
MATD3 algorithm implements the framework of centralized 
training with decentralized execution [11] which ensures all 
agents operate independently. The centralized training means 
that the input variables of critic network include the actions and 
states of all agents in training process, and the decentralized 
execution means that after finishing the training, only actor 
network with private observation is needed in the test 
environment. The framework guarantees that the learning 
process of the networks is stable, and all agents operate 
independently in testing. 
B. Implementation of the Proposed Method 
In this paper, the general-purposed MATD3 method is used 
to deal with the repeated game of electricity market equilibrium 
problem. Key elements of MATD3 method for solving the 
market equilibrium are stated as follows. 
1) Agent: Strategic GENCO bidders are the agents, who 
gradually learn the optimal bidding strategy by repeatedly 
interacting with the environment. 
2) Environment: The environment is defined as the process 
of electricity market clearing and carbon emission rights trading. 
After agents taking actions, the environment provides their 
rewards and the states for the next time step. 
3) State: The state is the output of each generator from the 
electricity market progress, and the private observation of each 
agent is its own output in the last game. 
4) Action: The action of agent is the bidding price of each 
GENCO bidder in one game, the action space of agent can be 
expressed as (7). 
5) Reward: The reward of agent is its profit in one market 
clearing process, considering its power generation cost, carbon 
emission cost and the income from electricity market and carbon 
emission trading market. The function of its reward can be 
expressed as (6).

two scenarios with higher and lower carbon quota, where the 
free carbon quotas are respective 50 tCO2 and 30tCO2, and the 
carbon price is 14 $/tCO2. We compare the carbon emission 
reductions of the two scenarios, which is shown in Table II. We 
can see that, in the context of CET, the scalar quantities of low 
carbon emission generators increase, and the market power of 
high carbon emission generators is weakened. Additionally, 
there is little carbon emission reduction in the scenario with 
higher carbon quota. Therefore, the carbon quota should be set 
appropriately lower, so that CET can better play its role.

The carbon price can also have an influence on the carbon 
reduction. We simulate and analyze the carbon reduction with 
the carbon price from 0$ to 20$. The result is shown in Fig. 1. 
The figure indicates that the carbon emission reduction does not 
always increase with the carbon price. Carbon emission 
reduction is greatest when the carbon price is around 15$, and it 
falls sharply when carbon price continues to rise. The main 
reason is that in the scenario with an excessive carbon price, low 
carbon emission generators are more inclined to profit from the 
carbon emission trading market by generating less electricity 
and selling more carbon emission rights, so they lack motivation 
to generate electricity. Therefore, the setting of carbon price 
should be carefully considered when designing CET mechanism.

B. Impacts on the Nodal Price 
Considering the CET market, the existence of carbon price 
increases the marginal cost of generation, which leads to a 
higher nodal price. We simulate the nodal price in the two 
carbon quota scenarios with the carbon price from 0$ to 20$, and 
the result is shown in Fig. 2. We can see that whether the free 
carbon emission quota is higher or lower, the nodal price tends 
to rise with the carbon price. An excessive nodal price can have 
negative effects on the electricity market, it increases both the 
generation cost of GENCOs and the electricity cost of users. 
This situation should be avoided because it seriously disrupts the 
market order.

V. CONCLUSION 
This paper proposes a multi-agent deep reinforcement 
learning algorithm for solving the electricity market equilibrium 
problem with multi-agent competition considering carbon 
emission trading. The electricity market equilibrium problem is 
formulated as a bilevel model. The upper-level model indicates 
power producers profit maximization problem, the goal of each 
GENCO bidder is to maximize its profit. The lower-level model 
indicates electricity market clearing model to minimize the total 
power generation cost of GENCOs. The CET market 
mechanism is taken into account in electricity market clearing 
model, where the influence of carbon price and free carbon 
emission quota on the electricity market are studied. The 
simulation result shows that an appropriately lower carbon 
emission quota can promote carbon reduction and increase the 
market competitiveness of low carbon emission generator. 
Additionally, the carbon price can raise the nodal electricity 
price, so the carbon price should be set appropriately low to 
reduce its impact on nodal electricity price. 
The research of this paper provides a reference for solving 
the electricity market equilibrium and help for CET market 
mechanism design, the proposed MATD3 algorithm can also be 
applied to solving other problems in the field of power system, 
such as optimal power dispatching problem and integrated 
energy system management. The model proposed in this paper 
can be extent to consider electricity spot market with multi-
timescale and multi-stage, and include other markets such as 
tradable green certificates market and consumption trading 
market.

A B S T R A C T
To address the increasing penetration of renewable energy sources (RES), battery energy storage systems (BESS) 
play an increasingly vital role in power systems, making it strategically important to investigate their bidding 
strategies for participation in electricity markets. However, traditional market models generally rely on perfectly 
rational agents and deterministic optimization, which limits their ability to capture the bounded rationality and 
risk preferences observed in real-world market behavior. To overcome these limitations, this paper proposes a 
novel multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) paradigm that integrates a risk-averse quantal response 
equilibrium (RQE) model into a multi-agent soft actor–critic (MASAC) architecture. The RQE formulation jointly 
embeds bounded rationality and risk-aversion into decision-making, enabling agents to respond smoothly to 
uncertain rival strategies while maintaining robustness against adverse market conditions. The proposed solution 
is assessed based on the IEEE 30-bus system under multiple different operational scenarios. Numerical results 
show that RQE-based MASAC achieves a higher overall reward than the baseline MASAC algorithm in approx­
imately 80% of the test scenarios. In the three extreme scenarios, BESS profit increases by 22.52%, 13.16%, and 
48.2%, respectively. These results demonstrate the value of jointly modeling bounded rationality and risk- 
aversion in strategic BESS bidding.
1. Introduction
The recent statistics released by the International Energy Agency(IEA) 
indicated that renewable energy will maintain strong growth by 2030 with 
an average annual growth rate of 8.4% [1]. To address the inherent inter­
mittency and variability of renewable energy sources (RES) such as wind 
and solar power, energy storage systems are crucial for achieving the 
seamless integration of renewable energy into power grids [2]. Among 
available storage technologies, battery energy storage systems (BESS) have 
become a more attractive solution for applications in power systems due to 
cost reductions [3]. BESS have significant advantages in power systems, 
including operational flexibility, fast response speed, high ramp rates, and 
no geographical restrictions [4,5]. To promote the sustainable development 
of BESS, the expanding scale of such systems should act as active market 
participants, generating profits and recovering investment costs through 
market-based behaviors [6].
A growing body of literature has investigated the participation of BESS 
in market transactions. Existing studies have examined the economic value 
of storage in arbitrage, reserve provision, frequency regulation, and multi- 
market coordination, and have developed various bidding strategies for 
day-ahead or real-time environments (e.g., [7–10]). In [7], a bi-level

methods are directly applied to multi-agent scenarios, the continuous 
changes in each agent's policy during training lead to non-stationarity of 
the environment from the perspective of an individual agent. In this 
regard, multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) has emerged as a 
promising approach for modeling strategic bidding behaviors in elec­
tricity markets. Recent studies have applied MARL to traditional gen­
erators (TG) (e.g., [11,18–20]), virtual power plants (VPP) (e.g., 
[17,21,22]) and electric vehicles (EV) (e.g., [13,23]), demonstrating its 
capability to capture dynamic interactions and learn near-equilibrium 
strategies from repeated market simulations. MARL methods such as 
multi-agent proximal policy optimization (MAPPO) [24], multi-agent 
deep deterministic policy gradient (MADDPG) [25], and multi-agent 
soft actor-critic (MASAC) [26] have been used to approximate market 
equilibrium under stochastic supply–demand conditions. Although 
these methods can learn strategic bidding policies under incomplete 
information, their policy stochasticity primarily supports exploration 
and is not, by itself, an explicit behavioral model. In particular, con­
ventional risk-neutral MARL maximizes an expected return and there­
fore cannot distinguish two behaviorally different effects: (1) bounded 
rationality, under which agents respond probabilistically rather than 
selecting an exact best response; (2) risk-aversion, under which agents 
place greater weight on unfavorable outcomes induced by uncertain 
rival bids. Modeling only the former may still expose an agent to adverse 
opponent actions, whereas modeling only the latter under a determin­
istic best-response assumption does not capture observed response 
dispersion. A unified equilibrium-learning framework is therefore 
needed to represent both effects within the policy update.
To address these methodological challenges, there is a growing need 
for learning frameworks that can simultaneously accommodate incom­
plete information, uncertainty in renewable and load forecasts, and 
realistic human-like decision behavior. According to behavioral eco­
nomics theory, the defining characteristics of human decision-making 
are imperfect optimization [27] and risk-aversion [28]. It is known 
that the inclusion of risk-aversion can explain the observed tendency of 
subjects to choose the safer strategy more often, as suggested in [29]. In 
addition, individuals are naturally prone to making mistakes and often 
fail to be perfectly optimal, known as bounded rationality, which is often 
captured in games through the idea of a quantal response equilibrium 
(QRE) [30]. Building on these findings, authors in [31] proposed a new 
natural equilibrium concept: risk-averse quantal response equilibrium 
(RQE), which could be easily calculated in matrix and finite-horizon 
Markov games. The results show that endowing artificial agents with 
human decision-making characteristics derived from behavioral eco­
nomics gives rise to an equilibrium in the game. Integrating such 
behavioral models into MARL can produce bidding strategies that are 
both robust and consistent with practical market behavior.
In addition to these behavioral considerations, applying MARL to BESS 
bidding introduces a unique modeling challenge: the state of charge (SoC) 
constraint couples decisions across all time periods, creating strong tem­
poral dependence in both state transitions and action feasibility. The bid­
ding strategy for any given hour directly affects the SoC in subsequent 
hours, which in turn constrains future charging/discharging capability and 
economic outcomes. When participating in day-ahead markets, BESS must 
determine a 24-h bidding vector in a fully coupled optimization landscape. 
It should be highlighted that conventional MARL modeling based on 
sequential multi-step Markov decision processes often struggles with such 
high-dimensional temporal coupling and often leads to slow convergence or 
infeasible action exploration. To overcome this limitation, this study adopts 
a 24-dimensional vector representation for both the action space and the 
state space, aligning the MARL decision with the market's unified 24-h 
clearing mechanism. By modeling the day-ahead bidding problem as a 
single-step Markov decision process (MDP) with vectorized decisions, we 
decouple temporal transitions while preserving the global inter-temporal 
constraints. This may significantly enhance training tractability and 
ensure that SoC constraints are respected across the entire scheduling 
horizon.
In summary, the key technical challenges requiring further in­
vestigations in the day-ahead bidding of BESS in the double-sided auc­
tion market can be stated as follows: (1) most existing game-theoretic 
models for BESS participation in day-ahead electricity market trans­
actions are established under the assumption of complete information, 
while relevant research that accounts for the dynamic bidding strategies 
of competing market participants remains scarce; (2) current MARL al­
gorithms applied under incomplete information conditions presume that 
all market entities are perfectly rational, with limited consideration for 
bounded rationality and risk-aversion preferences derived from behav­
ioral economics; and (3) within the bidding optimization framework, the 
all-day SoC constraints of BESS couple the decision-making processes 
across all time intervals, which poses significant computational chal­
lenges in solving the sequential continuous Markov game.
To this end, this paper proposes a risk-averse quantal response equi­
librium based optimal bidding solution for battery storage systems 
considering the double-sided auction market. In particular, a novel MARL 
paradigm is developed that integrates an RQE model into a MASAC archi­
tecture. Extensive experiments are conducted on an IEEE 30-bus system 
under multiple wind–solar generation and load forecast scenarios to eval­
uate the robustness and performance of the proposed method against 
benchmark MARL algorithms. The results demonstrate that the proposed 
approach achieves superior revenue stability, improved convergence 
characteristics, and enhanced adaptability to uncertainty compared with 
existing methods. The technical contributions made in this work are as 
follows: 
(1) A day-ahead double-sided auction market is established involving 
TG, RES, BESS, and retailers. The bidding task is formulated as a 
single-step vectorized MDP that resolves the intrinsic multi- 
period coupling caused by BESS SoC dynamics. This design en­
ables efficient convergence and realistic representation of stra­
tegic interactions across the full 24-h scheduling horizon.
(2) An RQE model is integrated into the MASAC architecture to 
jointly capture behavioral imperfections and risk-sensitive deci­
sion-making under incomplete information. The resulting RQE- 
based MASAC algorithm yields smoother equilibrium-seeking 
behavior, stronger robustness to uncertainty, and improved 
profit stability across diverse operating conditions.

outlines the proposed mechanism framework for BESS participation in 
the day-ahead double-sided auction market, as well as the correspond­

Numerical results and analysis are discussed in Section 4. Section 5

2. Problem formulation
2.1. Overview of the double-sided auction market
Fig. 1 illustrates the structure of the proposed double-sided auction 
market. The day-ahead electricity market consists of multiple heteroge­
neous participants interacting through a centralized market-clearing 
mechanism operated by an independent system operator (ISO). The main 
market participants include TG, photovoltaic panels (PV), wind turbines 
(WT), BESS, electricity retailers, and fixed loads. Before market clearing, 
generation-side agents submit their bidding strategies to the ISO. TG and 
RES primarily submit energy supply bids, while BESS participants submit 
both charging and discharging bids reflecting their operational flexibility. 
On the demand side, the retailers aggregate end-user demand and submit 
purchase bids representing expected electricity consumption, while fixed 
loads represent inelastic demand in the system. In the bidding strategy 
declaration, the declared electricity quantity of each agent is determined by 
the level of the declared bidding price.

After collecting bids from all market participants, the ISO performs a 
centralized day-ahead market clearing process subject to network and 
operational constraints. The clearing procedure determines the sched­
uled power output of generators, the charging or discharging schedules 
of BESS, and the cleared electricity purchases of retailers. At the same 
time, the market-clearing results generate locational marginal prices 
(LMP) that reflect the marginal cost of electricity supply at each node in 
the network. These LMP signals, together with the cleared power 
quantities, are then communicated back to all participants as market 
outcomes.

competition, and profound uncertainty. The traditional Nash Equilib­

capture the true strategic behaviors of market participants, who face 
severe risks from volatile clearing prices and the unpredictable bidding 
behaviors of their competitors. Therefore, RQE is considered appro­
priate for application in the multi-agent electricity market games. The 
RQE and the corresponding MASAC algorithm based on RQE are

3.2. RQE-based MASAC algorithm
To approximate the RQE of the electricity-market bidding game, we 
develop RQE-based MASAC. Baseline MASAC and RQE-based MASAC 
use the same CTDE architecture, stochastic actors, double-Q critics, and 
adaptive entropy temperature; they differ in the value used for the actor 
update. MASAC evaluates an action using the soft Q-value of a sampled 
joint action. RQE-based MASAC instead samples multiple joint actions 
from the rivals' current policies and aggregates the corresponding Q- 
values with an entropic risk operator. Because the operator assigns 
exponentially greater weight to low-Q outcomes, it discounts bidding 
actions that are profitable on average but vulnerable to plausible 
adverse rival bids.
The stochastic entropy-regularized actor provides a continuous 
quantal response: actions with higher utility receive greater probability, 
but the policy does not collapse immediately to a perfectly rational 
deterministic best response. The risk operator then modifies the action 
ranking by emphasizing unfavorable rival responses. For BESS, this 
mechanism balances transaction margins against the probability of 
market clearing. Under supply surplus or intense seller competition, for 
example, the learned policy may raise charging bids and lower dis­
charging offers, increasing the likelihood that both types of bids are 
accepted.

4. Case study
To verify the proposed market framework and algorithm, the IEEE 
30-bus standard test system is adopted to conduct a case study. The 
hardware platform for simulation operation includes an AMD Ryzen 
79,700 × 8-core processor (5.5 GHz), 48 GB RAM, and NVIDIA GeForce 
RTX 5080 GPU (16 GB). The software environment is Python 3.11.14: a 
reinforcement learning neural network is built based on the PyTorch 
framework, and the Gurobi ver.12.0.3 commercial solver is used to solve 
the electricity market clearing model.
4.1. Simulation parameters
In this section, the modified IEEE 30-bus network topology diagram 
is illustrated in Fig. 3. The simulation parameters of TG, RES, retailers 
and BESS are presented in Table 2 and Table 3, as suggested in [20]. The 
bidding limits of TG are determined by the linear marginal cost function. 
The upper and lower bidding limits for charging and discharging are set 
as 20–40 $/MWh and 12–24 $/MWh. The upper and lower bidding 
limits for RES are set at 10–20 $/MWh, and they default to bidding their 
predicted power. The upper and lower bidding limits for retailers are set 
at 15–40 $/MWh, and they determine their bidding quantity based on 
the load forecast value. The hyper-parameter settings of the RQE-based 
MASAC algorithm are shown in Table 4.
Forecast profiles for baseline demand, retailer demand, and RES 
output are constructed through scenario generation and clustering. The 
underlying hourly load and RES data are obtained from the PJM market 
[33], covering January 1, 2025, to December 31, 2025. A total of 3600 
original scenarios are generated using the generative adversarial 
network (GAN), and 60 representative scenarios are further obtained by 
the k-medoids clustering method [34]. Each hourly dimension is min- 
max normalized to [-1,1], after which a single GAN learns the joint 
distribution. The trained generator then produces 3600 joint daily sce­
narios. The parameters of the GAN are listed in Table 5. K-medoids 
reduction is then performed jointly rather than separately. A CLARA- 
style implementation evaluates ten random subsets of 3600 joint sce­
narios, retains the initialization with the lowest full-data assignment 
cost, and then refines the medoids using all 3600 scenarios. The number 
of clusters is set to 60.

4.2. Convergence analysis of each algorithm
To verify the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm, we compare it 
against several representative reinforcement learning methods, 
including MATD3, MADDPG, MASAC, and the proposed RQE-based 
MASAC. Fig. 5(a) illustrates the convergence curves of the reward 
functions during training for the different algorithms. The results show 
that, in the initial exploration phase, the traditional MADDPG and 
MATD3 algorithms exhibit unstable exploration due to the absence of 
entropy regularization. As a consequence, agents frequently adopt sub­
optimal actions, leading to noticeable fluctuations and occasional de­
clines in reward. In contrast, both MASAC and the proposed RQE-based 
MASAC demonstrate significantly smoother reward evolution during the 
exploration stage. This behavior highlights the intrinsic advantages of 
the MASAC framework: entropy regularization effectively balances 
exploration and exploitation, while the dual Q-network structure pro­
vides more stable value estimation.
During the convergence phase, MADDPG and MATD3 exhibit rela­
tively slow and unstable convergence. In contrast, MASAC and RQE- 
based MASAC demonstrate a much faster learning rate. It can also be 
observed that the RQE-based MASAC algorithm converges faster than 
the MASAC algorithm during the convergence process. This indicates 
that the RQE with the consideration of risk aversion can accelerate the 
convergence speed and reach the game equilibrium more quickly.
Fig. 5(a) shows that both MADDPG and MATD3 fail to converge 
within the total training episodes, indicating that these algorithms are 
unable to fully exploit the strategic value of the game under high 
complexity and uncertainty. In contrast, MASAC and RQE-based MASAC 
remain stable within a high-reward region, achieving approximately a 
50% improvement in returns compared with the former two methods, 
which demonstrates the superiority and stability of their learned stra­
tegies. Comparing MASAC with RQE-based MASAC, it can be observed 
that both algorithms converge to similar final reward levels. This in­
dicates that incorporating risk-aversion through the RQE mechanism 
does not degrade performance under standard multi-scenario 
conditions.
Fig. 5(b) presents the convergence behavior of the reward functions 
for several representative agents under the RQE-based MASAC algo­
rithm. As shown, the rewards of ES1 evolve smoothly with limited 
variation, whereas G1 and D1 exhibit substantial improvement as 
training progresses. This difference arises because the bidding strategies 
of G1 and D1 have a stronger impact on the locational clearing price at 
their respective nodes, requiring multiple rounds of learning to optimize 
their strategic behavior. In contrast, ES1 has limited ability to influence 
market prices due to its node location, capacity constraints, and 
charging/discharging power limits, resulting in relatively stable reward 
dynamics.

4.4.2. Profit under different scenarios
In this section, we conduct comparative experiments between the 
MASAC algorithm, which performs well during training, and the pro­
posed RQE-based MASAC algorithm. The models trained by the two 
algorithms are used for decentralized testing in the CTDE framework, 
and the total payoffs are compared across 60 different scenarios. The 
experimental results are presented in Fig. 8 and Table 10. The total profit 
of the RQE-based MASAC algorithm exceeds that of the MASAC algo­
rithm in 78.3% of scenarios (47 out of 60). Meanwhile, analysis of the 
average profits in Table 10 shows that the proposed RQE-based MASAC 
algorithm achieves higher average profits for all four types of units, 
namely TG, PV, WT, and BESS, compared with the MASAC algorithm. 
The average total profit is increased by $2825 (2.68%), demonstrating 
the favorable adaptability and robustness of the proposed method.
To further assess the risk-related performance, we evaluate the BESS 
profits over the 60 test scenarios using six statistics: the mean, standard 
deviation, minimum observed profit, 5th-percentile profit, and lower- 
tail conditional value-at-risk (CVaR) at the 10% and 5% levels. As 
shown in Fig. 9, the absolute standard deviation remains essentially 
unchanged, increasing only from 11.178 to 11.183. More importantly, 
the minimum observed profit increases from 0 to 0.761, while the 5th- 
percentile profit increases from 1.466 to 2.788. The lower-tail CVaR at 
the 10% level increases from 1.152 to 2.223, and that at the 5% level 
increases from 0.467 to 1.167. The results support improved downside- 
profit robustness and mean-normalized stability.
4.4.3. Market-clearing and line load results under different scenarios
To evaluate the adopted single-step formulation under operating 
conditions that stress both the network and the intertemporal BESS 
constraints, two scenarios are selected from the test set. Scenario #3: its

hourly load ramp ranks first; Scenario #4: its daily mean PV output 
ranks third, while both its PV peak and maximum hourly PV ramp rank 
first.

substantially more BESS charging and discharging. The two clearings

materially greater congestion. The bids nevertheless remain network-

binding, but a converged feasible clearing cannot violate those limits.

4.4.5. Sensitivity analysis
To investigate the influence of the risk-aversion coefficient, a sensi­
tivity analysis is conducted by varying τ from 0.5 to 1.1 while keeping 
the other algorithmic parameters unchanged. The trained policies are 
evaluated under the three extreme cases. The average total reward of all 
market participants and the average combined reward of BESS are used 
as performance indicators. The sensitivity of the Monte Carlo risk 
evaluation to the number of opponent-action samples K is further 
summarized in Table D1.
As shown in Fig. 15 and Table 12, the influence of the risk-aversion 
coefficient is nonlinear. When τ is relatively small, the adversarial dis­
tribution is strongly restricted by the KL-divergence penalty, and the 
risk-adjusted policy remains close to the nominal expected-reward pol­
icy. Increasing τ gives greater weight to unfavorable competitor re­
sponses and encourages the BESS agents to submit more conservative 
and clearing-oriented bids. The average BESS reward increases within 
the moderate range of τ and reaches its highest value at τ = 0.8.
The average total reward reaches its maximum at approximately τ =
1.0, whereas the BESS reward decreases markedly at this value. This 
result reveals a trade-off between system-level reward and the profit­
ability of the focal BESS agents. An excessively large risk-aversion co­
efficient allows the adversarial distribution to deviate further from the 
nominal rival policy, leading the BESS agents to overemphasize unfa­
vorable outcomes and potentially sacrifice profitable bidding opportu­
nities. Therefore, τ = 0.8 is selected because it provides the highest 
average BESS reward while maintaining the total market reward within 
a high and stable range. This value achieves an appropriate balance 
between downside-risk protection and profit-seeking behavior.
5. Conclusions and future work
This paper presents an RQE-based MASAC framework for modeling 
optimal bidding strategies of BESS in the day-ahead double-sided auc­
tion market. The primary contributions are threefold. First, a compre­
hensive market structure is constructed that integrates strategic 
interactions among TG, RES, BESS, and electricity retailers under 
network constraints. Second, the proposed learning architecture embeds 
risk-aversion and bounded rationality into MARL through the RQE 
formulation, providing a more realistic representation of market 
decision-making behavior under incomplete information. Third, the 24- 
dimensional single-step MDP design effectively resolves the intrinsic 
temporal coupling imposed by SoC constraints and enables efficient 
convergence of BESS bidding strategies.
Extensive simulations on the IEEE 30-bus system verify the effec­
tiveness of the proposed method. Compared with other MARL algo­
rithms, the MASAC and RQE-based MASAC algorithms exhibit 
significantly superior convergence performance. In day-ahead elec­
tricity market bidding, the agents trained by the RQE-based MASAC 
algorithm demonstrate strong risk-aversion capabilities under uncertain 
conditions, e.g., low demand levels, and achieve higher total profits 
across 60 test scenarios than the baseline algorithms. In the three 
extreme simulation cases, the proposed algorithm outperforms the 
baseline algorithms in terms of total profit in 84.7%, 73.3%, and 78.3% 
of the scenarios, respectively, with the average profit of BESS increased 
by 22.52%, 13.16%, and 48.2%, verifying the strong adaptability and 
robustness of the proposed method.
Nevertheless, the current framework has several limitations. The 
behavioral and risk-related parameters are primarily determined 
through numerical experiments, while the underlying behavioral as­
sumptions have not yet been sufficiently validated using real-world 
market data. In addition, the simulations are conducted on a relatively 
small test system and are limited to a day-ahead market framework. 
Future research will therefore focus on calibrating and validating the 
proposed model using historical bidding records and market-clearing 
data. Scalable learning architectures, including parameter sharing, 
mean-field MARL, hierarchical MARL, and distributed or federated 
training, will also be explored to improve its scalability to larger power 
systems with more numerous and heterogeneous market participants, e. 
g., VPPs and EVs. The framework can be extended to the coordinated 
participation of BESS in energy, reserve, and frequency regulation 
markets by considering coupled capacity constraints, service-stacking 
decisions, battery degradation, regulation deployment, and multi- 
timescale market clearing. Employing advanced artificial intelligence 
tools, e.g., Large Language Models (LLMs), to support decision-making 
for trading entities also represents a promising research direction.

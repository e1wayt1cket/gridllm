A B S T R A C T
Singapore power grid is planning to import clean power such as photovoltaic (PV) power generation from 
overseas through subsea cables. To participate in the Singapore electricity market, the power output from PV 
plants is required to be constant during each bidding period and hence battery storage systems (BSSs) need to be 
deployed to compensate PV power fluctuations. This paper proposes a learning-based bidding strategy to 
maximize the expected profit of such a PV-BSS plant. The overall bidding process is modelled as a Markov 
Decision Process (MDP) and the optimal bidding strategy is acquired through deep reinforcement learning (DRL), 
considering PV power revenues, penalty payments for power shortage, and battery health degradation cost of the 
BSS. To more accurately characterize the actual operational behavior of the BSS, a detailed BSS model is built to 
estimate the state of power (SoP) and state of health (SoH) of the BSS. Numerical case studies are carried out to 
demonstrate that the proposed method can achieve promising profits compared to classic optimization-based 
methods and preserve BSS with less SoH degradation.
1. Introduction
With the global target for a low-carbon and sustainable energy 
future, Singapore's power sector has set the target to realize net-zero 
emissions by 2050 [1]. For decarbonization, the Energy Market Au­
thority of Singapore has proposed several strategies, one of which is to 
“pursue the adoption of electricity imports to access cleaner and cost- 
effective energy sources beyond Singapore's borders”. In this context, 
overseas photovoltaic (PV) plants have been planned for importing 
electricity into Singapore in the future [2].
To participate in the electricity market of Singapore, PV power plants 
(electricity importers) are required to provide bidding offers every 
dispatch period and maintain a non-intermittent power supply during 
each dispatch period [3]. However, in practice, the PV generation is 
highly volatile and hardly predicted. To compensate the intermittent PV 
generation, a battery storage system (BSS) needs to be deployed in the 
PV power plant. To maximize the profit of the PV-BSS plant, its optimal 
bidding is an emerging problem.
In the literature, several optimization-based bidding strategies have 
been proposed [4–7], targeted at wind power plants, electric vehicle 
aggregators, networked microgrids, etc. Targeting at the overseas PV- 
BSS bidding problem, our recent work [8] reported an optimal power 
bidding approach, which optimized the offered power of overseas PV 
plants to maximize the profit through a stochastic programming 
approach. However, most of these bidding methods neglected the 
operational characteristics of the BSS, such as its battery health degra­
dation and maximum available power output that are critical in 
providing high and frequent charging/discharging power in the 
wholesale electricity market. Besides, optimization-based methods rely 
on accurate PV forecasting and uncertainty modelling of the forecasting 
error and may also suffer from computational challenges during online

In recent years, deep reinforcement learning (DRL) has attracted 
considerable interest in solving complex decision-making problems. 
Learning from interactions between the environment and the agent, DRL 
can address complex problems with massive state spaces. In the field of 
power systems, DRL has been applied in frequency/voltage control [10], 
energy management [11], optimal power flow [12], etc. For the bidding 
strategy in the electricity market, [13] utilized DRL to design a strategic 
bidding approach for producers. [14] proposed a DRL-based bidding 
strategy for EVAs under the information asymmetry in the local energy 
market. In [15], a risk-averse bidding strategy was proposed for virtual 
alliances in the active distribution network, learned by a multi-agent 
DRL algorithm. In [16] [17], proximal policy optimization (PPO) was 
used for energy bidding in real-time energy market. [18] designed a 
multi-agent DRL framework for a photovoltaic plant with an energy 
storage system participating in multi-timescale electricity markets. 
However, those DRL-based bidding methods are designed for specific 
use cases, which require pretrained forecast modules for price, renew­
able energy generation [13,14,17], and cannot be directly applied for 
PV-BSS bidding in Singapore electricity market due to its unique market 
rules. Further, since BSS has become a crucial role in the energy bidding 
market, more detailed battery models should be considered in the 
learning-based energy arbitrage, which is insufficiently studied in the 
existing research.
Besides, the operational characteristic of the BSS has a significant 
impact on the PV-BSS plant's bidding performance. Hence, the state of 
the BSS should be accurately and comprehensively estimated, such as 
state of charge (SoC) [19], state of health (SoH) [20], state of power 
(SoP) [21], etc. In [22], SoC management was taken into consideration 
for energy management, which aims to realize an optimal battery energy 
planning. Besides, battery degradation models were built to estimate 
BSS's SoH in [23,24]. Compared to SoC and SoH, very limited studies 
have been reported for SoP, which indicates the ultimate capacity of 
charging/discharging power of an BSS. Conventionally, the maximum 
BSS charging/discharging power is determined by its rated power, but in 
practice, the real available power (SoP) may be influenced by different 
factors, such as SoC, terminal voltage, current [25]. Hence, it is critical 
to consider SoP in the energy bidding, enabling more accurate bidding 
and reliable operation of the BSS. Existing SoP estimation methods can 
be categorized into model-based and data-driven ones. For the former 
[26] [27], an equivalent circuit model (ECM) can be built to emulate to 
reflect battery's dynamic behavior and calculate power capabilities with 
the current/voltage limits. For the latter [28,29], the mapping rela­
tionship between SoP and measurements (SoC, temperature, etc.) can be 
extracted through machine learning, which relies on the quality and 
quantity of the training data. On the other hand, existing SoP estimation 
methods mainly focus on battery cell level and SoP for large-scale BSSs is 
rarely studied in the existing research. Besides, the detailed BSS 
modelling and state estimation (SoH, SoP) are generally neglected in the 
field of energy bidding, which shall be further studied to couple with the 
bidding strategy.
To fill above research gaps, this paper proposes a novel learning- 
based bidding strategy for overseas PV-BSS plants participating in 
Singapore Electricity Market. The proposed bidding agent utilizes his­
toric BSS SoC and PV power output data as the input to determine the 
optimal bidding power of the plant, with the objective of maximizing 
revenue and avoiding economic penalties. The main contributions of 
this paper are summarized as follows: 
1) First of all, a detailed BSS model is developed to accurately and 
comprehensively characterize the BSS. Based on the model, the SoP 
and SoH of the BSS can be estimated during operation. Note that the 
contribution does not focus on proposing a new BSS model, but 
rather in integrating a realistic and constraint-aware BSS model into 
a DRL framework tailored for the Singapore electricity market.
2) The bidding process of the PV-BSS plant is modelled as a Markov 
Decision Process (MDP) according to Singapore electricity market 
rules, which is then trained by the PPO algorithm. Once trained in

2. Problem description and modelling
Fig. 1 illustrates a typical overseas PV-BSS power plant connected to 
Singapore Grid. The PV-BSS plant utilizes the solar panel to transform 
solar energy into electricity, which can be stored in the BSS device or be 
sold to the power grid directly through the inverter. As an intermediate, 
BSS interacts closely with the PV plant via charging/discharging. In this 
section, the bidding problem formulation and BSS modelling will be 
explained in detail.
2.1. Bidding problem formulation
In Singapore Electricity Market, which only has real-time market, 
overseas PV-BSS plants are required to provide their electricity offer for 
sale in every dispatch period (30 min), and the submission window for 
the bidding will be closed 65 min before the dispatch commencement. 
Besides, to take communication and processing time into consideration, 
it is normal to reserve 10 min for the bidding submission. As a result, for 
conventional optimization-based bidding methods, the lead time of PV 
forecast shall be at least 75 min [8]. This bidding process is illustrated in 
Fig. 2.
This paper assumes the PV-BSS plants to be a price-taker, so only 
power quantity is bided. Therefore, the bids of PV-BSS plant will not 
affect the uniform price in this study. In this regard, we do not model the 
uniform pricing mechanism and only consider wholesale electricity 
market. Such assumption is appropriate given Singapore Electricity 
Market structure, where prices are largely determined by system-wide 
combined-cycle gas turbine generation and are not materially affected 
by the bidding of individual renewable units. Moreover, the plant 
operates as an overseas asset under a fixed export contract and is not 
permitted to engage in strategic price-setting behavior, which further 
supports the non-price-making assumption. Note that other electricity 
markets can also be considered by specifying their bidding requirements 
as the parameter settings to the proposed method.
After the bidding submission, the offered power must be consistent 
during the 30-min dispatch period. To smooth PV fluctuation, BSS will 
release/absorb the power to maintain the total power output at the 
offered amount. Once the offered amount cannot be satisfied, the PV

3. Proposed learning-based method
This paper designs a learning-based bidding strategy for PV-BSS 
power plant. First, the overall bidding problem is modelled into a 
Markov Decision Process (MDP). Then, the general framework of the 
proposed methodology is explained. After that, policy learning process 
based on Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm is introduced.
3.1. Markov decision process (MDP) modelling
The optimization problem (1)–(9) are highly nonlinear and non- 
convex. As an alternative of classic optimization-based methods, DRL- 
based methods can solve nonlinear/non-convex problems through the 
interaction between environment and agent. To apply DRL in this bid­
ding problem, the main technical challenge includes adapting the bid­
ding strategy to Uniform Singapore Energy Price, handling the 
uncertainty of solar generation under tropical weather conditions, and 
aligning with the market's 30-min settlement intervals. To handle these 
technical issues, the overall problem is formulated as a Markov Decision 
Process (MDP), which is introduced as follows.

command of bidding power) based on the observed state s(t) as a =
π(s|θ), where θ indicates the parameters of π. After action a(t) is 
executed in the environment, a reward r(t) can be obtained to evaluate 
the performance under the pair of {s(t), a(t)}. Based on this process, the 
overall bidding problem is formulated as an MDP:

4. Case study
To validate the proposed methodology, several case studies are 
carried out in this section. The PV generation data are obtained during 
the time range from 22-Oct-2017 0:00 to 31-Dec-2017 23:30, with a 
time resolution of 1 min. In our case, the use of 71-day data was pri­

seasons, and irradiance as well as demand patterns remain relatively 
stable throughout the year. Consequently, PV output and price dynamics 
during October–December are highly representative of typical operating 
conditions. To exemplify, the PV generation from the first seven days are 
plotted in Fig. 5. Besides, electricity price data from National Energy 
Market of Singapore (NEMS) are obtained for training and testing, as 
shown in Fig. 6. Besides, the parameters of the PV-BSS power plant are 
summarized in Table 1. All tests are conducted by Matlab R2020b

In this section, the BSS model is firstly validated based on the

connected strings and M = 1500 series-connected cells per string. The 
polynomial parameters of electric elements (voc, R0, R1, C1) in (12)–(15) 
are from [37], and the curves of these polynomial functions are pre­

Table 2. Based on (18), the terminal voltage vb can be calculated ac­

which is plotted in Fig. 8. If a different type of battery is used, its 
nameplate parameters are needed, which can be obtained from battery

Based on (26), (27), the SoP-SoC relationship is obtained and shown 
in Fig. 9. As presented in Fig. 9 (a), discharging SoP will be determined 
by SoC constraint if SoC is low, since BSS does not have enough stored 
power to discharge. When SoC increases, discharging SoP will be 
determined by voltage constraint to ensure that the terminal cell voltage 
may go beyond the allowable range, i.e. SoP = SoPvb = vb,max•in(SoC(t), 
vb,max). Besides, there is a certain range (SoC is around 12–20 %) that the 
current through strings may go beyond the maximum allowable range. 
So, in such range, discharging SoP is determined by the current 
constraint, i.e. SoP = SoPin = vb(SoC(t), id,max)•id,max. After that, SoP can 
be obtained directly as the rated maximum discharging power PDis,max, 
since other constraints are not significant.

4.2. Offline training
After system modelling, offline training is conducted based on PPO 
algorithm, searching for an optimal bidding strategy. According to MDP 
defined above, the actor network (bidding agent) is built by two DNNs 
with the structure of 90 × 512 × 128 × 1, for mean and covariance 
respectively in (39). Besides, the critic network (state function) is also 
built with the structure of 90 × 512 × 64 × 1. For initialization, these 
DNNs are assigned random weights and biases. The activation functions 
of hidden layers are set by “relu” function in both actor and critic. The 
activation functions of output layers are set by “relu” function in the 
critic and “tanh” function in the actor. At the beginning of each learning 
episode, PV historic data are randomly selected for policy exploration. 
During the learning episode, the bidding agent runs for multiple itera­
tions and the transition {s(t), a(t), p(t), r(t), s(t + 1)} is stored into a 
memory cell, which is implemented in Matlab interface. After several 
episodes, a number of cells can construct as a replay buffer. To update 
the policy, several memory cells are selected from the replay buffer to 
update the agent's parameters. Through numerous learning episodes, the 
bidding agent can be well-trained under various PV generations and is 
applied to online applications. Note that the agent will not be trained at 
online stage and only conduct matrix calculations, which can signifi­
cantly reduce the online computational burden. According to our tests, 
the online computational time is only 2.470 ms, which is fully 
compatible with online applications. The parameters for offline training 
are listed in Table 3.

overall training process. To show the clear trend of episode rewards,

rithms are also tested, which reflects the better performance of the 
adopted PPO. Although SAC has a good capability of policy exploration 
as an entropy-based algorithm [38], the learning process is unstable,

4.3. Test results
After multiple iterations of training, the bidding agent can be applied 
to online stage. To validate the performance of the proposed bidding 
strategy, several case studies are given in this section. It should be noted 
that the overall dataset contains PV data of 71 days, where the data of 
first 40 days are used as training dataset and the remaining 31-day data 
(day 41–71) are used for testing. Therefore, training and testing datasets 
do not overlap. 
1) Test Case of Proposed Method
Fig. 11 presents the test case within one week (from day 50 to day 
56). In Fig. 11 (a), the proposed agent can follow variable PV genera­
tions and generate the bidding power command by historic information, 
which does not need PV power forecasting. A comparative analysis is 
provided to explain the necessity for SoP and SoH modelling. Since the 
real SoP does not match the rated maximum power according to the 
discussion above, the BSS output may exceed real-time SoPs if not 
considering SoP, as shown in Fig. 11 (b). Taking SoP into consideration, 
the real output power of BSS shows a relatively lower level. Besides, the 
variation of SoC also reflects the smoother operation of BSS when 
considering SoP in BSS modelling. During the operation, SoC can be kept 
within the constraints [5 %, 95 %]. In the end of daytime (19:00–20:00), 
BSS's SoC is restored to 50 %. For SoH modelling, the initial SoH is 
defined as 99 %, indicating the ratio of the remaining BSS capacity. By 
taking the cost of SoH capacity into the reward given in (35), the 
degradation can be improved during operation, as presented in Fig. 11
(d). 
2) Comparison with Classic Optimization Methods
To further explain the effectiveness of the proposed method, it is 
compared with classic model (optimization)-based methods. The opti­
mization problem is formulated by (1) as a mixed-integer linear pro­
gramming (MILP) model that can be efficiently solved by off-the-shelf 
solvers, where the time resolution is also defined as 30 min.
The benchmark optimization model is formulated as a two-stage 
stochastic programming and solved by the sampling average approxi­
mation (SAA), which is derived from [8] and does not consider SoP and 
SoH. The first-stage decision variable is revealed before the uncertainty, 
which corresponds to PBid; the second-stage variables include PDis and 
PCh, depending on PBid and the realization of uncertainty. Assuming the 
PV forecasting error follows a specific probabilistic distribution such as 
normal distribution or beta distribution, the SAA samples a finite 
number of possible scenarios from its probabilistic distribution, and the 
expectation of the objective function can be approximated as the 
average of that of the sampled scenarios. In case study, the optimization 
model is solved by Gurobi, using 100 samples. The solution accuracy 
follows Gurobi's default tolerance settings, including a feasibility toler­
ance of 1 × 10−6, optimality tolerance of 1 × 10−6. To ensure the long- 
term profit, SoC is required to restore to 50 % in each time horizon (30 
min). Note that this definition “50% SoC at the end of each 30min ho­
rizon” is only for optimization-based benchmarks, which is not included 
in the proposed method, and it is reasonable that SoC is required to 
restore to 50% in each time horizon. First, if SoC is not required to 
restore to 50% in each time horizon, BSS cannot be charged timely by 
the optimization-based method for real-time bidding. Second, to restore 
SoC to 50% in each time horizon (30 min) can still fully utilize PV 
generation and will not sacrifice any potential profits. Besides, the 
optimization-based baseline emulates realistic forecast conditions by 
adding controlled levels of Gaussian noise to the real PV data, which 
mimics forecast errors with varying accuracy. More details of the 
benchmark optimization model can be found in [8].
In the tests, historical data of day 42–48 are used. First, the bidding 
results of the proposed and optmization-based method are presented in 
Fig. 12 (a), where the optimization-based method utilizes real PV data as 
the input. As shown, these two curves have a similar trend as PV vari­
ation. SoC curve is also given in Fig. 12 (b). Compared to the 
optimization-based method, which makes limited use of BSS, the pro­
posed method is capable to utilize BSS within a larger range of SoC. 
Moreover, the average profits per 30 min are plotted in Fig. 12 (c). The 
proposed method can achieve similar profits with the optimization- 
based method during the operation. It should be noted that in this 
case, true PV data is used for the optimization-based method.
However, true PV power outputs are unavailable at real-time stage in

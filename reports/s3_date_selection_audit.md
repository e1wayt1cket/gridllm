# S3: date selection audit

Why the ledger's date cannot be chosen the way it was, what the dates actually
hold, and what would have to be true before one is designated.

**No date is designated by this report.** `2022-10-14` is retained as a
historical audit sample and **withdrawn as a default**: the builder no longer
picks a date at all, and now requires `--date` explicitly.

> **Update, 2026-10-15 — a rule was adopted, and it selects 2022-11-30.**
> `2022-10-14` stays withdrawn. What changed is that the single-day diagnostic
> case needed *a* rule rather than a hand-picked date, and the rule adopted is
> **`earliest_complete_date`**: the earliest date, in calendar order, on which
> every mapped bus is covered for all 96 periods. It is not a function of any
> quantity of load, which is the whole objection this report raised against its
> predecessor. `build_industry_node_loads.select_load_date` implements it and
> `--date-from-audit` applies it; the chosen date and the audit's sha256 go into
> the ledger's manifest.
>
> It is **not** a typical-day rule. A date chosen by its position on the
> calendar carries no claim about being average, and the report below stands:
> the rankings in this file are coverage ranks and not load quantiles.
> See `reports/single_day_diagnostic_case.md`.

## The rule that was withdrawn

The builder chose "the date with the most complete qualified meters, earliest
wins ties". That rule is deterministic, which is why it looked safe, and it is
**biased**: a date with more reporting meters carries more load, so the rule
selects from the high end of the distribution and then that day is described as
representative. Bus 16 shows the size of the effect.

`build_industry_node_loads.pick_date` is kept, because the audit of this bias is
reproducible through it, and a caller may still ask for it by name. A ledger may
not.

## Bus 16: is 208 MW on the chosen date comparable with the 40 MW average?

Asked directly, the four things that could make two numbers incomparable:

| | 730-day figure | 2022-10-14 figure | same? |
|---|---|---|---|
| user set | 2 users | 2 users | **yes** |
| meter set | 7 meters | 5 of those 7 have a reading that day | **no — fewer meters contribute** |
| time granularity | 96 slots x 0.25 h | 96 slots x 0.25 h | **yes** |
| aggregation | convert each meter-day, sum by user, then by bus | identical | **yes** |
| denominator | 730 days x 24 h | 1 day x 24 h | **different things** |

The two denominators are in fact the same *kind* of quantity here, because bus
16 has a qualified reading on all 730 days: 703,036 MWh / (730 x 24 h) =
**40.13 MW**, and the same total over the days it reports on is also 40.13 MW.

So the gap is **not** a formula error, a different user set or a different
granularity. It is date selection, and the selection landed at the top:

| | bus 16, daily mean |
|---|---|
| 2022-10-14 | **208.11 MW** |
| rank among 730 days | **694th** (top 5%) |
| p75 day | 57.91 MW |
| median day | **0.01 MW** |
| p25 day | 0.00 MW |

**The median day on this bus is 0.01 MW.** Half its days are effectively zero —
the all-zero pattern the screening already flags for 钢铁. So on bus 16 the
choice of date moves the daily mean by four orders of magnitude, between a day
that is nearly nothing and a day that is near the maximum. Any single date is a
strong claim about this bus, and "the most complete one" was the strongest claim
of all.

## What the dates actually hold

`reports/date_selection_audit.csv` carries one row per real date with load
completeness, users and buses covered, the buses missing, the reading counts,
and a price note. It is regenerated by
`python src/build_industry_node_loads.py --audit-dates`.

| | |
|---|---|
| dates with a load reading | 730 (2021-01-01 to 2022-12-31) |
| **dates on which every mapped bus has data** | **30 (4.1%)** |
| dates with at least one mapped bus missing | 700 |
| buses covered per date | min 12, median 14, max 17 (of 17 mapped) |
| meters with a complete day, per date | min 40, median 60, max 86 |
| readings that could not be converted | 2 of 45,736 |
| **dates with a dated real price curve** | **0** |

The 30 complete dates are **all in December 2022** — 2022-11-30 to 2022-12-31,
with two gaps. They are not a sample of the year; they are the one stretch where
every mapped bus reports at once.

The buses that keep dates from being complete:

| bus | industry | dates missing it |
|---|---|---|
| 32 | 数据中心 | 698 of 730 |
| 29 | 印刷业 | 627 |
| 1 | 商业综合体 | 566 |
| 28 | 机械 | 81 |
| 22 | 水泥制造 | 12 |

**No date has a real price curve to align with.** The only dated real price
curve is `261009`, covering 2026-10-09, which is entirely outside the load's
range; the other three are window means with no dates recorded. So the
completeness table above is a load-side table, and the price side is empty for
every row of it. Nothing was copied onto a date to fill it, and no synthetic
price was substituted.

## Why the rankings are not load quantiles

The audit reports **coverage** ranks: how many meters reported, how many buses
are covered. It deliberately does **not** report where a day falls in a load
distribution.

While the reading unit is `unit_unverified`, a cross-user aggregate is a sum of
numbers whose unit no source states. Ranking days by that sum ranks them by an
unverified quantity, and a percentile computed from it would be quoted as a
physical demand level it may not be. The bus 16 figures above are reported
because they are the reconciliation the gate asked for — the same meters, the
same formula, the two totals — and they are labelled as that, not as a load
distribution.

## The rule for choosing a date, and what it waits on

**Answered on 2026-10-15.** This section used to say no rule had been adopted
and that the choice was still open. The rule now in force is
`earliest_complete_date`, described at the head of this report: take the dates
in ascending calendar order and return the first on which the load data is
whole. It replaces the completeness-ranked rule, whose bias is the subject of
the first two sections, and it removes the need to decide on any load quantity.

What it does **not** answer is whether the 30 whole dates — all in December 2022
— are a study period anybody wants. That remains a scope question, and the
single-day case answers it only for itself, by being one diagnostic day rather
than a period.

The three preceding sections are unchanged and still govern how a chosen date
must be *read*: the composition on a high-coverage day is not the composition on
a median day, and this workbook's ratings do not admit a load percentile.
## What this changes in the artefacts

- `2022-10-14` remains the date of the existing audit ledger and is described as
  a historical audit sample, not a representative day.
- `python src/build_industry_node_loads.py` without `--date` now refuses, with
  the reason, instead of silently choosing the biased one.
- `--audit-dates` writes the per-date table.
- `reports/date_selection_audit.csv` is new.

Nothing was scaled, discarded, copied or synthesised, and the unit gate, the
user-level mapping and the raw-data protections are unchanged.

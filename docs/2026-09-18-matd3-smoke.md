# `smoke_matd3.py`: environment and policy instrumentation, 2 seeds x 60 episodes

`src/smoke_matd3.py` is an **instrument, not a training run.** It walks the B3
facilities through the environment for a fixed number of episodes and looks
directly at the failures a reward curve cannot show — a run that has collapsed
to the truthful corner still produces a flat, plausible-looking reward. What it
does *not* do is train: it never calls `trainer.remember()`, so the replay
buffer stays empty, `MATD3.update()` returns before its first gradient step, and
every quote it records comes from the seeded actor initialization plus
exploration noise. The recorded run below carries `final_critic_loss: null` on
both seeds, which is that fact stated in the artifact itself.

This distinction matters for how the numbers below may be read. They are
measurements of the **environment and the action box** — that the box is
reachable, that the clearing is stable, that no quote escapes its bounds, that
the SOC constraints hold. They are not evidence about a *learned* policy, and
the section [What each check actually established](#what-each-check-actually-established)
separates the two.

## What each check actually established

**Terminal SOC equality** is checked directly, not inferred: the day is
re-settled through `counterfactual_eval.settle_day` and each unit's final SOC is
compared with its initial one. The gap over both seeds is exactly 0.0. This is a
property of the settlement and the clearing, and it holds for any declared
quotes — it is the check on the *mechanism*, and it would hold equally for a
trained policy.

**Valid actions and valid SOC are close to tautological here, and are recorded
as such.** Before the warmup threshold the quotes are drawn by
`np.random.uniform` between the box bounds, so "inside the box" is true by
construction; afterwards they come from a `tanh`-squashed actor, which is inside
the box by construction too. The SOC bounds are enforced by the clearing rather
than by the declared quotes. These checks would catch a real defect — a box
miscomputed, a clearing that returns an infeasible schedule — but passing them
is not evidence that any policy is behaving.

**No corner collapse** is the weakest of the seven for the same reason. Mean
normalized deviation is 0.50–0.53 and only 0.1% of quotes sit within 2% of the
truthful corner, but an untrained actor initialized near the middle of its box
produces that without any learning. What the measurement does show is that the
box is *reachable* — the retired logit-quadratic penalty, whose gradient grew
with distance from the reference, could not have let a policy sample there — so
it is a check on the parameterization, not on the policy.

**Stable clearing** is a genuine environment result: 1440 blocks per seed, all
cleared, zero fallbacks.

## The policy-level checks live elsewhere

Because this instrument does not train, the action-box, SOC-bound and
charge/discharge-exclusivity checks that *are* about a trained policy come from
replaying a finished run's saved policies, not from here:
`src/audit_protocol_smoke.py`'s `check_policy_behaviour`. See
`reports/protocol_smoke_v0.1_lambda010.md`, which reports 0 of 240 replayed
blocks out of box, 0 SOC violations and 0 simultaneous charge/discharge for the
protocol smoke's trained fleet.

The twelve `state of charge` / `actions` rows this instrument writes are equally
not a policy trace: they record what the seeded actor did, and a formal run's
per-episode artifacts are what a training-progress claim should be read from.

## One observation to carry into the full run

**The fleet's state of charge never rises above its initial 0.5 in either seed**
— the range is exactly `[0.100, 0.500]` both times, while `soc_max` is 0.90.

This is an environment observation rather than a training one, and it is
conditional on the price map it was measured under: the same instrument over
`synthetic_v1` reaches `[0.160, 0.615]` and `[0.160, 0.672]`, so the fleet does
charge when the wholesale curve gives it a reason to. See
`docs/2026-09-20-external-wholesale-price.md`. The retired net-load price map
this table was measured under gave the seeded actor no reason to charge, and an
untrained actor has no other one.

**The full run must show the SOC rising above 0.5 on some days, or the fleet is
not arbitraging and the thing being measured is not what the research is
about.** Worth an explicit check in the first full run rather than assuming it
follows from the price map alone.

## Quote spread: a property of the initialization, not of learning

The calibration's frozen-critic probe leaves a quote spread of 65.4 CNY/MWh at
`lambda = 0.25`; this instrument reports 21–23 CNY/MWh. Neither number is a
converged policy's spread. The calibration's probe holds a fixed arbitrary
critic and asks where it settles the actor; this instrument samples a seeded
actor with exploration noise and never updates it. The two are different
quantities and the gap between them is not evidence about either.

It does mean the `non_degenerate_actions` threshold (1 CNY/MWh) is a weak one —
at 21 CNY/MWh the quotes span about 14% of the discharge box and 7% of the
charge box. **The full run should report the spread per evaluation so a
continuing collapse is visible as a trend rather than only as a threshold
crossing.**

## Why the instrument is left as it is

Adding the missing `trainer.remember()` call would turn this into the training
smoke its name suggests. That is a change to a script whose historical results
are already written down, and it is a decision rather than something to do
silently — a repaired instrument and an unrepaired one produce different numbers
under the same command. It is recorded here so the next reader does not have to
rediscover it from a `null` in the JSON.
